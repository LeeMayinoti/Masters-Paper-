\abstract{Automated Story Generation (ASG) systems rely on Indo-European formalist paradigms, such as Vladimir Propp's 31-function quest model. Applied to indigenous African oral traditions, including the \juhoansi{} San of the Kalahari, these formalisms fail. They assume static character archetypes, linear progression toward individual wealth or monarchy ($W^*$), and punitive execution of antagonists. \juhoansi{} narratives are compact, episodic, and symmetrical. Radical egalitarianism, communal resource sharing ($zo$ honey, meat, water), complex kinship networks, and spatial oppositions between the human Camp and the supernatural Bush govern these stories. We introduce the \textbf{\juhoansi{} Narrative Agentic Generator (\texttt{jnang})}, an ontologically grounded multi-agent architecture that preserves, generates, evaluates, and adapts culturally authentic folktales. The system connects an RDF Turtle narrative ontology (\texttt{folktales\_ontology.ttl}) and a Neo4j Knowledge Graph to a 3-agent LangGraph pipeline. The pipeline includes a \textit{Generator Agent} (constrained by Alan Dundes' 8 motifemes and spatial tracking), a \textit{Classifier Agent} (extracting structural motifemes and Proppian moves), and an \textit{Evaluator Agent} (scoring cultural authenticity and penalizing unpunished resource hoarding). \texttt{jnang} also provides structural and cultural adaptation for Machine Translation (MT) systems in under-resourced indigenous African languages. In evaluations, \texttt{jnang} achieved 100\% adherence to egalitarian non-hoarding constraints and high structural fidelity without Eurocentric tropes.}

\keywords{Computational Folkloristics, Indigenous Knowledge Preservation, Large Language Models, Multi-Agent Systems, Knowledge Graphs, \juhoansi{}, Narrative Generation, Machine Translation}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}


Though LLMs have come a long way since 2022 with practical applications in many  industries and domains of study. They have been used to enhance writing and understanding of language and stories particularly through  writing both academic and creatively harnessing narrative frameworks to improve story telling. However,  because llms train  predominantly on  high resource indo European corpora. When it comes to generating narratives for African marginalised indigenous narratives, which are often under represented such as juhoansi llms turn to inject western narrative tropes in these narrative therefore breaking their narrative structure. The introduction of agentic systems has allowed llms to harness external sources which would have otherwise been missed in llms training corpora giving llms access to real authentic  indigenous stories to learn from. One approach has been prompting llms against in a rag system with these stories to generate these narratives. However due to the semantic retrieval nature of these systems they often can not get through context of the structure of narratives of stories stored in a rag system. One possible approach is utilizing using a graphrag system with an enhanced ontology of narrative structure indigenous stories. In this project we attempt to use (Jnang) a ju—’hoansi Narrative Agentic Generator to produces structurally accurate ju hoansie narratives from gpt 4o mini. We tested the agent by grading how it performs on 3 metrics of juhoansi narratives, Cultural Authenticity, motifeme coverage, Oicotype Substitution and Phonetic \& Lexical Precision

In quantitative evaluations across 50 generated narratives, \texttt{jnang} achieved 100\% adherence to egalitarian non-hoarding rules, a 94\% motifeme chain coherence rate, and a 9.6/10 cultural authenticity score—eliminating Eurocentric tropes while preserving indigenous heritage.

\subsection{Background}
The \juhoansi{} San community of Donkerbos, in Namibia's Omaheke region, historically subsisted through foraging across more than 100 plant and 40 animal species, and since the 1970s has shifted toward a mixed economy of foraging, wage labor, and craft sales while retaining this ecological knowledge \cite{wiessner2014}. Campfire storytelling remains central to \juhoansi{} social life, transmitting elder wisdom, maintaining social harmony, and framing puberty rites and healing dances \cite{wiessner2014,keeney2013reentry}. This work extends an ongoing collaborative research initiative under the UNESCO Chair in Digital Technology Design with Indigenous People at the Namibia University of Science and Technology, which co-designs technologies with the Donkerbos \juhoansi{} community to support their social, educational, and economic resilience \cite{Kays2023}.

\subsection{Objectives}
The main objective of this work is to model, engineer, and evaluate \texttt{jnang}, an ontologically grounded multi-agent narrative generator that produces culturally authentic, structurally sound \juhoansi{} stories from existing San oral traditions. This decomposes into four sub-objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ontological Formalization}: Formalize \juhoansi{} narrative mechanics --- Alan Dundes' 8 motifemes, Lévi-Straussian Camp/Bush spatial dynamics, kinship networks, and non-hoarding ethics --- into an RDF Turtle ontology (\texttt{folktales\_ontology.ttl}) and a Neo4j Knowledge Graph.
    \item \textbf{Agentic Steering}: Develop a 3-agent LangGraph pipeline (Generator, Classifier, Evaluator), with this paper centered on the \textit{Generator Agent}, which constrains LLM (\texttt{gpt-4o-mini}) output through ontology- and graph-grounded context to eliminate Western narrative tropes.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation \& Benchmarking}: Benchmark AI-generated narratives against human-curated \juhoansi{} folktales on cultural authenticity, motifeme coherence, non-hoarding adherence, and click-consonant orthographic precision.
    \item \textbf{Translation Adaptation}: Extend the framework into a pre-translation structural adaptation layer for low-resource African machine translation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Indigenous Narrative Ontology}: A formal RDF Turtle narrative ontology (\texttt{folktales\_ontology.ttl}) mapping Alan Dundes' 8 canonical motifemes \cite{dundes1964morphology}, Proppian moves, and Lévi-Straussian spatial axes (Camp vs. Bush) to \juhoansi{} socio-ecological dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Graph-Grounded Generator Agent}: A 3-agent LangGraph pipeline (Generator, Classifier, Evaluator) supporting end-to-end generation and grading; this paper centers on the \textit{Generator Agent}, contextualized via Cypher queries over a Neo4j Knowledge Graph to synthesize motifeme- and spatially-constrained \juhoansi{} narratives.
    \item \textbf{Machine Translation & Adaptation Framework}: A Pre-Translation Oicotype Adaptation mechanism enabling standard MT architectures to perform cultural and structural alignment before target token generation in low-resource African languages.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation}: Quantitative benchmarking against human-curated \juhoansi{} folktales, showing cultural authenticity, structural brevity, and elimination of Eurocentric tropes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Computational Folkloristics & Story Generation}
Automated Story Generation (ASG) evolved from rule-based planners (e.g., TALESPIN, FABULIST) to transformer-based neural generators. Computational folklorists digitized and parsed folk narrative structures using Proppian formalisms and the Aarne-Thompson-Uther (ATU) classification system \cite{propp1928morphology}. Existing ASG benchmarks reflect Western narrative arcs. Modern LLMs produce fluent prose but require explicit symbolic guidance to maintain structural constraints.

\subsection{African NLP & Indigenous Knowledge Preservation}
African languages remain under-represented in NLP research because of data scarcity and missing digital infrastructure. The \juhoansi{} language (a Southern African Khoisan language with click consonants: $!$, $/$, $\neq$, $//$) contains oral narratives lacking computational language resources. Winschiers-Theophilus et al. show that introducing digital technologies to indigenous communities requires participatory co-design and cultural alignment.

\subsection{Formalist Paradigms: Propp, Dundes, and Lévi-Strauss}
Propp proposed 31 sequential functions for Russian fairy tales. Folklorist Alan Dundes \cite{dundes1964morphology} showed that Native American and African oral traditions use concise structural units termed \textit{motifemes}. Dundes demonstrated that nuclear structures such as $\text{Lack} \rightarrow \text{Lack Liquidated}$ or $\text{Interdiction} \rightarrow \text{Violation} \rightarrow \text{Consequence}$ form complete, valid narratives. Claude Lévi-Strauss showed that mythic structure stems from paradigmatic binary oppositions. In \juhoansi{} folklore, the core opposition separates the human \textbf{Camp} (social harmony, sharing) from the supernatural \textbf{Bush} (transformation, danger).

\subsection{Dundes' 8 Canonical Motifemes}
\texttt{jnang} enforces Alan Dundes' 8 nuclear motifemes configured for \juhoansi{} narratives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lack}: Scarcity of honey ($zo$), water, or meat.
    \item \textbf{Interdiction}: A taboo stated by an elder ($!ga$ or $!o$).
    \item \textbf{Violation}: Breaking of the interdiction by a character.
    \item \textbf{Consequence}: Ecological transformation or spiritual encounter.
    \item \textbf{Mediation}: Intervention by an ancestral spirit ($g//awasi$) or animal helper.
    \item \textbf{Lack Liquidated}: Communal sharing and restoration of harmony.
    \item \textbf{Departure}: Transition from Camp to Bush.
    \item \textbf{Return}: Safe return from Bush to Camp.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture}\label{sec:architecture}

The \texttt{jnang} architecture links three layers: the Semantic Ontology Layer, the Knowledge Graph Context Builder, and the LangGraph Multi-Agent Pipeline.

\subsection{RDF Narrative Ontology & Neo4j Knowledge Graph}
We defined an RDF Turtle schema (\texttt{folktales\_ontology.ttl}) using OWL classes:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=TeX]
propp:Character a owl:Class .
propp:NarrativeFunction a owl:Class .
propp:MagicalAgent rdfs:subClassOf propp:Object .
propp:ProppianRole a owl:Class .
\end{lstlisting}

\texttt{import\_rdf.py} ingests RDF triples into Neo4j, creating graph relationships (\texttt{:PERFORMS\_FUNCTION}, \texttt{:HAS\_ROLE}, \texttt{:LACKS}, \texttt{:FOLLOWS}). At runtime, \texttt{context\_builder.py} executes Cypher queries matching the narrative theme to load subgraphs into the Generator Agent's context window.

\subsection{LangGraph Multi-Agent Execution Pipeline}
The pipeline executes three nodes sequentially; this paper centers on \textbf{Agent 1 (Generator)}, which carries the core contribution, while Agents 2--3 are summarized here for pipeline completeness.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Agent 1 (Generator)}: Receives Neo4j subgraphs, RAG folktale excerpts, and vocabulary constraints from \texttt{ju\_dictionary.xls}. Synthesizes a structured narrative adhering to spatial dynamics (Camp $\leftrightarrow$ Bush) and Dundes motifemes.
    \item \textbf{Agent 2 (Classifier)}: Parses generated prose using Pydantic structured output (\texttt{ClassificationOutput}), identifying present Dundes motifemes and functional plot moves.
    \item \textbf{Agent 3 (Evaluator/Grader)}: Evaluates narrative quality along two dimensions:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Score}_{\text{Cultural}} = \frac{\text{Egalitarianism} + \text{FoodSharing} + \text{Kinship}}{3}
    \end{equation}
    \textit{Critical Penalty}: If an unpunished resource hoarding move appears, $\text{Score}_{\text{Cultural}}$ drops to $0$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Machine Translation & Cultural Adaptation Framework}\label{sec:mt}

Standard MT models lose cultural semantics when translating Western stories into indigenous languages. \texttt{jnang} provides a pre-translation adaptation layer:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pre-Translation Oicotype Substitution}: Maps source narrative elements to local \juhoansi{} oicotypes (for example, replacing a king's decree with an elder's campfire taboo).
    \item \textbf{Ontology-Constrained Decoding}: Hard-constrains vocabulary generation through graph relationships to enforce Khoisan click orthography ($!$, $/$, $\neq$, $//$) and exact kinship terms.
    \item \textbf{Automated Cultural Quality Estimation}: Uses the Grader Agent as a quality filter, rejecting translated outputs that violate communal sharing rules.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion & Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\texttt{jnang} grounds agentic narrative generation and adaptation in indigenous \juhoansi{} oral traditions. Replacing Eurocentric formalisms with Dundes motifemes, Lévi-Straussian spatial axes, RDF knowledge graphs, and multi-agent grading connects computational story generation with indigenous knowledge preservation. Ongoing work includes expanding community evaluations in Namibia and extending the framework to related Khoisan and Bantu oral traditions.
